Slop Audit

Checks a site, not a single article. It samples pages across the archive, scores each one, then looks at what they have in common — because the giveaway is rarely one page, it's the sameness across all of them.

How many pages to sample

Pages come from the site's own sitemap, sampled across the whole archive rather than the newest few. Sites with no sitemap fall back to homepage links.

This tab never leaves your browser. Use it for sites behind a login, or when the scan comes back empty.

The audit shows up here.

Every page gets its own score, then the site gets one. The site-level checks are the useful part: phrases repeated verbatim across articles, articles that all land at the same length, scores that cluster too tightly, missing bylines, and posts published in suspiciously tight bursts.

This is not proof. No tool can establish that text was written by AI. A site run by people working from a template can score high, and edited AI content can score low.

A handful of pages is also a sample, not the site. Treat a high score as a reason to read closely yourself, not as a conclusion.
